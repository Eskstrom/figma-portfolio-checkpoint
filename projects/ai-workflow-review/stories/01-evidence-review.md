# Story 1: Making AI-generated information easier to verify

[Project overview](../README.md) · [Full case study](../CASE-STUDY.md) · [Next story](02-review-confirmation.md)

## Situation

As the sole designer, I worked on a healthcare review experience where operations teams checked information extracted from clinical documents. We connected with hospital technology and operations partners to understand how the review tools fitted into their processes.

## Observation

During review, operations partners moved repeatedly between an extracted value and the original document. Even reasonable-looking values needed supporting evidence. Checking several fields meant searching the packet and finding their place again. Reducing data entry had not removed the effort of verification.

## Design decision and collaboration

I brought source documents and editable output into one workspace, with source references, highlighting, and a refocus control. Reviewers could inspect the evidence while retaining access to the information they were checking.

Technology partners helped connect available source-location information to the interface. Operations partners helped establish whether the evidence shown was useful for the decision. This grounded the interaction in how the task was performed.

![Source evidence beside editable fields](../assets/03-preview.png)

## Outcome

Reviewers could move between values and supporting evidence with less searching and fewer interruptions. Verification and correction became part of the same working context.

## Learning

AI output becomes useful when people can evaluate it. The effort required to verify and correct an answer is a core part of the design, alongside presenting the answer itself.

## Artifact note

The public extraction screens show the shared workspace, source-link icons, and refocus control. Document content is synthetic. Outcomes are qualitative.
